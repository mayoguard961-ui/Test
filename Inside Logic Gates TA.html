    Tidy an expression in 3 moves

    Three signs to know first:

    +

    OR: at least one is true. “tea + coffee” = you drink tea, or coffee, or both.

    ·

    AND: both are true. Two things written side by side, like PQ, also mean AND. Brackets next to each other are joined by AND.

    P

    NOT: the bar means the opposite. P is 1 when P is 0. The bar stays glued to its letter.

      The fast way · 3 shortcuts

      Remember
      1. Fill: cut the Out column into blocks of 4, lay each block as a row, swap the 3rd and 4th box. (4 inputs: also swap the 3rd and 4th row.)
      2. Circle: biggest first: 8, then 4, then 2, then 1. Edges touch.
      3. Read: look at the labels of the group. Same digit → keep the letter (1 = A, 0 = A). Different digits → delete the letter.

      Simplify with algebra, then check with a K-map

      The key move is the combining rule: when two terms are the same except for one letter, that letter disappears. XY + XY = X(Y + Y) = X · 1 = X. When one term can pair with two others, copy it first (X = X + X).

      The method in 4 actions
      1. Find two terms that are the same except for ONE letter.
      2. If one term is needed by two partners, copy it (X = X + X).
      3. Take out what the pair has in common (distributive law).
      4. The letter and its opposite make 1 (X̄ + X = 1), so that letter disappears.
